Indori poha, dal bafla, kadhi-chawal, bhutte ka kees: central Indian food is simple and filling. The trick is keeping the poha without the jalebi, and the bafla without the bowl of ghee.

What makes the difference in Central Indian food

  • Poha yes, jalebi noA plate of poha is about 270 kcal. It is the jalebi beside it that doubles breakfast.
  • Ask for less ghee on baflaDal bafla is traditionally soaked in ghee. With a teaspoon instead of a ladle it fits a weight-loss day.
  • Sprouts and chana are cheap proteinA cup of sprouts is about 10 g of protein for 125 kcal.
  • Kadhi-chawal: more kadhi, less chawalKadhi is light; the rice is where the calories are.
  • Snack on roasted chanaRoasted chana or makhana beats namkeen for both protein and calories.

How the numbers are worked out

Your daily calorie need is estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, adjusted for activity. For weight loss we subtract 500 kcal a day — roughly 0.45 kg a week — and never go below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women. Protein is set at 1.8 g per kg of body weight, using an adjusted weight above a BMI of 25 so the target stays realistic. Every calorie figure is an approximation of a typical home-cooked portion.

Questions people ask

Can I lose weight eating Central Indian food?

Yes. Weight loss comes from eating somewhat fewer calories than you burn, consistently — not from a particular cuisine. Keep the Central Indian meals you like, control the portion of rice or roti and the cooking fat, and get enough protein.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

It depends on your age, height, weight, sex and how active you are. The free planner works it out with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula and subtracts 500 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.45 kg a week. It never goes below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women.

Is poha good for weight loss?

Yes. A plate is about 270 kcal and keeps you full. It is low in protein (about 5 g), so add boiled chana or peanuts, and skip the jalebi.
