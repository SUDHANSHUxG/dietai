How to make a weight-gain day work

  • Keep the surplus smallThe planner adds 300 kcal a day to what you burn. A much bigger surplus adds more fat than muscle.
  • TrainExtra food builds muscle only alongside strength training. The planner's Build muscle goal assumes you train.
  • Get protein at every mealThese days give 113–163 g. The planner sets your own goal at 1.6 g per kg of body weight and adds a top-up if your meals fall short.
  • Use easy extrasA cup of toned milk is 120 kcal, a banana 105, 30 g of peanuts 170 and a roti 120 — the extras the planner uses when meals alone fall short.
  • Weigh in once a weekBody weight swings day to day with water and food. One weigh-in a week, at the same time of day, shows the real trend.

Questions people ask

How many calories do I need to gain weight?

About 300 kcal a day more than you burn. The free planner works out what you burn from your age, height, weight, sex and activity (the Mifflin-St Jeor formula) and adds 300 kcal when you pick Build muscle — for a 22-year-old man, 175 cm and 60 kg, who trains 3–5 times a week, that is about 2,763 kcal.

Can I gain weight on a vegetarian Indian diet?

Yes. The vegetarian day above reaches 2,776 kcal with 118 g of protein from everyday foods like dal, paneer, curd, chana and milk.

What can I add to eat more?

When your meals fall short of your target, the planner adds a cup of toned milk (120 kcal), a banana (105 kcal), 30 g of peanuts (170 kcal) or a roti (120 kcal), and counts them in your day.
