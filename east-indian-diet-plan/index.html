East Indian diet plan for weight loss

Rice and fish, dal-bhaat, litti-chokha, sattu: the food of Bengal, Bihar and Odisha has excellent cheap protein in it. The two things to manage are the size of the rice mound and the fried sides that come with it.

What makes the difference in East Indian food

  • Portion the bhaatA cup and a half of cooked rice is about 308 kcal. Keep the fish or dal the same and trim the rice first.
  • Fish is your best friendCooked fish is about 25 g of protein per 100 g for roughly 175 kcal. A light macher jhol is close to an ideal weight-loss meal.
  • One fried side, not threeBhaja, fries and pakoras in mustard oil add up fast. Pick one per meal.
  • Sattu is cheap proteinA glass of sattu drink is about 180 kcal with 10 g of protein — one of the best-value breakfasts in India.
  • Count the mishtiSweets are fine; just count them as the calories they are rather than as an extra.

How the numbers are worked out

Your daily calorie need is estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, adjusted for activity. For weight loss we subtract 500 kcal a day — roughly 0.45 kg a week — and never go below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women. Protein is set at 1.8 g per kg of body weight, using an adjusted weight above a BMI of 25 so the target stays realistic. Every calorie figure is an approximation of a typical home-cooked portion.

Questions people ask

Can I lose weight eating East Indian food?

Yes. Weight loss comes from eating somewhat fewer calories than you burn, consistently — not from a particular cuisine. Keep the East Indian meals you like, control the portion of rice or roti and the cooking fat, and get enough protein.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

It depends on your age, height, weight, sex and how active you are. The free planner works it out with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula and subtracts 500 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.45 kg a week. It never goes below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women.

Is fish curry good for weight loss?

Usually, yes. Cooked fish gives about 25 g of protein per 100 g for around 175 kcal. Keep the gravy light on oil and watch the rice served with it.
