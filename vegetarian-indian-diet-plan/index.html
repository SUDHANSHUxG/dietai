How to make a vegetarian day work

  • Put a pulse at the centre of two mealsA cup of cooked dal is about 18 g of protein, rajma 13 g, chole 13 g. Two cups of them across lunch and dinner give about 26–36 g before anything else on the plate.
  • Add curd or chaas to one mealA cup of curd is about 8 g of protein for 150 kcal, and it goes with almost every Indian meal.
  • Measure the paneer100 g of paneer is about 18 g of protein but 265 kcal. It is a good protein; just weigh it for a week so it doesn't quietly double.
  • Soya chunks top the tableSoya chunks (30 g dry): about 16 g of protein for 104 kcal — more protein per calorie than anything else in the table above.
  • Count the cooking fatA teaspoon of oil or ghee is roughly 45 kcal. A heavy tadka can add 100–200 kcal that no recipe card shows.

Questions people ask

Can a vegetarian Indian diet give enough protein?

Yes, with some planning. The three days above give 60–72 g of protein from meals alone at 1,500 kcal. Dal, rajma, chole, paneer, curd, tofu, sprouts and soya chunks are the main sources. The free planner sets your own protein goal from your weight and, where the meals fall short, suggests a top-up counted inside your calories.

How many calories should a vegetarian eat to lose weight?

The same as anyone else: it depends on your age, height, weight, sex and how active you are, not on whether you eat meat. The free planner works it out with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula and subtracts 500 kcal a day. It never goes below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women.

Is paneer good for weight loss?

Paneer is high in protein — about 18 g per 100 g — but also about 265 kcal per 100 g, more than most other vegetarian proteins. It fits in measured portions. Tofu gives similar protein for fewer calories: 16 g for 145 kcal per 100 g.
