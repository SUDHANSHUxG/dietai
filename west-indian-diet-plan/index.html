What makes the difference in Gujarati and Maharashtrian food

  • Notice the sugarMany Gujarati dals and kadhis are cooked with sugar or jaggery. Asking for it to be halved changes very little in taste and a lot in calories over a week.
  • Steamed farsan over friedDhokla is steamed — two pieces are about 150 kcal. Khakhra is also light. Fried farsan is the expensive one.
  • Bajra and jowar rotlaMillet rotlas and bhakri are filling and fit well; keep the ghee on them small.
  • Chaas with every mealA glass of buttermilk is about 60 kcal and helps with fullness.
  • Konkan fish for proteinIf you eat fish, a fish fry or curry with solkadhi is a strong, high-protein lunch.

How the numbers are worked out

Your daily calorie need is estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, adjusted for activity. For weight loss we subtract 500 kcal a day — roughly 0.45 kg a week — and never go below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women. Protein is set at 1.8 g per kg of body weight, using an adjusted weight above a BMI of 25 so the target stays realistic. Every calorie figure is an approximation of a typical home-cooked portion.

Questions people ask

Can I lose weight eating Gujarati and Maharashtrian food?

Yes. Weight loss comes from eating somewhat fewer calories than you burn, consistently — not from a particular cuisine. Keep the Gujarati and Maharashtrian meals you like, control the portion of rice or roti and the cooking fat, and get enough protein.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

It depends on your age, height, weight, sex and how active you are. The free planner works it out with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula and subtracts 500 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.45 kg a week. It never goes below 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 kcal for women.

Is dhokla good for weight loss?

Yes, it is steamed rather than fried — two pieces are about 150 kcal with 6 g of protein. Watch the sweet chutney and the tempering oil.
